## Question

How should I start a research project on how the human mind focuses on a task?

## Short Answer

Begin by clarifying your research goal, collecting foundational sources on attention and task focus, then iteratively build a structured wiki that captures key concepts, entities, claims, open questions, and your own synthesis.

## Detailed Synthesis

1. Define the scope
   - Decide whether you want to study cognitive attention, task management, distraction resistance, or the neuroscience of focus.
   - Write a concise research question such as: "What mechanisms allow humans to maintain sustained focus on a task?"

2. Gather and ingest sources
   - Collect high-quality materials: books, review papers, experiment summaries, and reputable articles on attention.
   - Add each source to `raw/sources/` and create a corresponding wiki source page in `wiki/sources/`.

3. Identify core concepts and entities
   - Extract recurring terms like sustained attention, selective attention, cognitive load, prefrontal cortex, and executive control.
   - Create dedicated pages in `wiki/concepts/` and `wiki/entities/` so the ideas become reusable.

4. Build synthesis pages
   - Use analysis pages in `wiki/analyses/` to answer specific questions, compare models, and summarize evidence.
   - Link analyses back to source and concept pages.

5. Track open questions and gaps
   - Keep a running list of unanswered questions in the wiki and the issue tracker.
   - Use those gaps to guide deeper reading and new source collection.

6. Iterate with experiments or reflections
   - If you want practice, add applied notes on how you personally maintain focus, test different techniques, and compare them with research.
   - Use the documentation process itself as a learning tool.
